Our history & culture

BARMM’s identity is shaped by Indigenous communities, Islam, trade across Southeast Asia, local sultanates, colonial encounters, and continuing efforts toward self-determination.

A living history

Heritage that keeps moving.

The Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) is located in the southern Philippines. Its communities have long-standing connections with maritime trade and neighboring parts of Southeast Asia. Islam became deeply rooted in parts of Mindanao and the Sulu Archipelago centuries before Spanish colonial rule reached much of the Philippines.

Bangsamoro history includes powerful sultanates, distinct languages and customs, and communities that maintained their identities through changing political eras. The region’s story also includes conflict, displacement, peacebuilding, and the pursuit of meaningful autonomy.

BARMM was established in 2019 following the ratification of the Bangsamoro Organic Law. The region continues to develop its institutions while preserving cultural heritage and working toward peace, opportunity, and inclusive progress.

Festivals & gatherings

Celebrate with respect.

Festivals and community gatherings vary by locality and calendar. Depending on the place and year, visitors may encounter cultural showcases, local celebrations, religious observances, and events highlighting traditional arts.

Travel tip: Confirm dates with official local tourism offices and follow guidance on dress, access, and photography.

Crafts & food traditions

Made by hand. Shared with care.

  • Inaul weaving: a traditional textile associated with Maguindanaon communities.
  • Maranao okir: flowing decorative motifs found in traditional arts and design.
  • Traditional kulintang music: gong-based musical traditions among several communities.
  • Regional cuisine: dishes and cooking practices that reflect local ingredients, heritage, and family traditions.
